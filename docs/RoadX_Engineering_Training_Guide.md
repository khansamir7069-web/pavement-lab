## 1. Pavement Design Philosophy (IRC:37-2018)
IRC:37-2018 guides the design of flexible pavements using a mechanistic-empirical (M-E) framework. Pavements are modeled as multi-layered elastic structures under a standard dual-wheel configuration. Design evaluation involves two core criteria:
1.  **Bituminous layer fatigue cracking** (tensile strain at the bottom of the bituminous layer, $\varepsilon_t$).
2.  **Subgrade rutting** (vertical compressive strain at the top of the subgrade, $\varepsilon_v$).

---

## 2. Mathematical Core & Structural Equations

### 2.1 Traffic Projections (Cumulative MSA)
Design traffic is calculated as cumulative Million Standard Axles (MSA) over the design life ($n$):
$$N = \frac{365 \cdot A \cdot [(1+r)^n - 1]}{r} \cdot D \cdot F \cdot 10^{-6}$$
Where:
*   $N$ = Cumulative design traffic (MSA)
*   $A$ = Initial commercial traffic (CVPD) after construction $= P \cdot (1+r)^x$ (where $P$ is count traffic and $x$ is years between count and construction completion)
*   $r$ = Annual traffic growth rate decimal
*   $n$ = Design life (years)
*   $D$ = Lane Distribution Factor (LDF)
    *   Single-lane roads: LDF = 1.0
    *   Two-lane single carriageway: LDF = 0.50 (each direction)
    *   Four-lane single carriageway: LDF = 0.40
    *   Dual carriageway 2-lane: LDF = 0.75
    *   Dual carriageway 3-lane: LDF = 0.60
    *   Dual carriageway 4-lane: LDF = 0.45
*   $F$ = Vehicle Damage Factor (VDF), converting commercial vehicles to equivalent standard axles.
*   *Linear Case:* When growth rate $r = 0$, traffic accumulates linearly:
    $$N = 365 \cdot A \cdot n \cdot D \cdot F \cdot 10^{-6}$$

---

### 2.2 Subgrade Support (Resilient Modulus, $M_r$)
Subgrade resilient modulus ($M_r$) represents soil stiffness under repeated wheel loads. Per **IRC:37-2018 Annex E**:
*   For soaked CBR (%) $\le 5.0\%$:
    $$M_r = 10 \cdot CBR \quad (\text{in MPa})$$
*   For soaked CBR (%) $> 5.0\%$:
    $$M_r = 17.6 \cdot CBR^{0.64} \quad (\text{in MPa})$$
*   *Soil Cap Check:* Soil with soaked CBR $< 2.0\%$ must be replaced or stabilized with lime/cement.

### 2.3 Mechanistic Verification Strain Criteria
Pavements are evaluated at critical strain points under dual-wheel load (20,000 N per wheel at 0.8 MPa contact pressure).

#### Fatigue Life of Bituminous Layers ($N_f$)
Fatigue life represents the number of standard axle repetitions allowed before 20% cracking of the pavement surface. Sourced from **IRC:37-2018 Eq. 3.1 & 3.2**:
$$N_f = 0.0571 \cdot \beta_f \cdot \left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_t}\right)^{3.89} \cdot \left(\frac{1}{E}\right)^{0.854}$$
Where:
*   $\varepsilon_t$ = Tensile strain at the bottom of the bituminous layer.
*   $E$ = Elastic modulus of the bituminous mix (MPa).
*   $\beta_f$ = Fatigue calibration factor (depends on binder type and air voids).

#### Rutting Life of Subgrade Soil ($N_r$)
Rutting life represents standard axle repetitions allowed before 20mm rut depth accumulates:
$$N_r = 1.41 \cdot 10^{-8} \cdot \beta_r \cdot \left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon_v}\right)^{4.533}$$
Where:
*   $\varepsilon_v$ = Vertical compressive strain at the top of the subgrade.
*   $\beta_r$ = Rutting calibration factor.

## 3. CTB/CTS Stabilized Design Principles
Stabilized pavements introduce a Cement-Treated Base (CTB) and a Cement-Treated Sub-base (CTS) layer to increase load-bearing capability. Sourced from **IRC:37-2018 Section 9**:

### 3.1 Layer Configuration & Modulus
*   **Bituminous Cover:** Sized to protect the stabilized slab from traffic wear and moisture infiltration.
*   **CTB Modulus:** Sourced from laboratory flexural strength testing, typical design range: 3000 to 10000 MPa.
*   **CTS Modulus:** Design range: 1000 to 4000 MPa.
*   **GSB Layer:** Placed under the CTS to provide drainage and construction platforms.

### 3.2 Cracking Risks
Cement-treated materials are highly brittle and susceptible to:
1.  **Shrinkage Cracking:** Occurs during cement hydration. Requires strict moisture control.
2.  **Reflection Cracking:** Cracks in the CTB slab migrate upwards into the bituminous cover. Avoided by using SAMIs (Stress Absorbing Membrane Interlayers) or a granular sandwich layer.
3.  **Tensile Fatigue:** Bottom tensile strain in the CTB slab must not exceed flexural capacity.

## 4. Marshall Mix Design (Volumetrics)
Marshall mix design determines the Optimum Binder Content (OBC) by checking the volumetric properties of compacted asphalt mixes (per **MORTH Section 500 / IRC:111**).

### 4.1 Volumetric Equations
*   **Air Voids ($V_a$):**
    $$V_a = \left(1 - \frac{G_{mb}}{G_{mm}}\right) \cdot 100$$
    Where $G_{mb}$ is bulk specific gravity and $G_{mm}$ is maximum theoretical specific gravity of the compacted mix.
*   **Voids in Mineral Aggregate ($VMA$):**
    $$VMA = 100 - \frac{G_{mb} \cdot P_s}{G_{sb}}$$
    Where $P_s = 100 - P_b$ is the aggregate percentage and $G_{sb}$ is the combined aggregate specific gravity.
*   **Voids Filled with Bitumen ($VFB$):**
    $$VFB = \left(\frac{VMA - V_a}{VMA}\right) \cdot 100$$

### 4.2 OBC Spline Fitting
RoadX utilizes a cubic spline interpolation model to fit volumetric parameters against binder content ($P_b$). The OBC is determined as the binder percentage where $V_a$ is exactly equal to 4.0% (target air voids), provided the mix satisfies MORTH limits for stability ($> 9$ kN), flow (2-4 mm), and VFB (65-75%).

## 5. Engineering Screening Compatibility Rules
The **Engineering Intelligence Checker** monitors layer layouts against compatibility guidelines:
*   **Base-to-Subbase Modular Ratio:**
    $$E_{\text{base}} / E_{\text{sub-base}} \le 4.0$$
*   **Stiff Layer Support:** Stiff stabilized base ($E \ge 3000$ MPa) must not sit directly on granular support $< 500$ MPa, to prevent slab cracking.
*   **Stiffness Inversions:** Lower layer elastic modulus must not exceed $1.3 \cdot E_{\text{upper}}$, preventing unbonded plate action.
*   **Poisson's Ratio:** Restricted to realistic material bounds (granular = 0.35, stabilized = 0.25, bituminous = 0.35).
